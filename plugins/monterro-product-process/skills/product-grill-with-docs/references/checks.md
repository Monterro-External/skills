# Readiness checks

The readiness checklist of the Monterro product ticket standard at **v1.14**, as pass/fail rules. This file is the standard the grill uses unless `standard` in the config names a company's own. Numbers are stable; skills and evals refer to them. Applies-to column: S means the check runs for S-tier tickets; all others run for M and L only.

Where a configured `standard` and this file disagree, the configured standard wins: say so in the verdict's Notes rather than grading against both.

| # | Check | Passes when | Applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intent | One sentence, describing one change. Two verbs joined by "and" is usually two changes | S M L |
| 2 | Evidence | Every claim in Problem and evidence has a link or an attachment. The original signal is present verbatim, with source, author and date. "Link needed" or a named-but-unlinked source fails | S M L |
| 3 | Outcome and success criteria | The outcome is stated as changed customer behaviour rather than as something shipped. At M that is one line of prose and there is nothing further to grade. At L each success criterion is also measurable with the bar as a number, says nothing about technology, and describes something a user could observe; a sample size is not a threshold, and no criterion may promise what a product decision elsewhere in the ticket makes impossible | M L |
| 4 | Non-goals | Populated, and at least one names something a reasonable reader would otherwise assume is in scope | M L |
| 5 | No implementation | Nothing from the prohibition list (modules, services, schemas, endpoints, frameworks, file names, implementation steps, estimates, resourcing) as a requirement; kept ideas are marked non-binding. An analytics event named as a decision is **not** implementation: section 5 of the standard lists "analytics events to emit" among the decisions product owes engineering, so naming one passes and naming none is the thing worth a Note | S M L |
| 6 | Scenarios | Every acceptance scenario set includes an error case and a regression (upset-if-broken) case | S M L |
| 7 | No vague adjectives | In the author's own prose anywhere in the ticket, Problem and evidence included: none of fast, scalable, secure, intuitive, robust, seamless, or their kin, without a number beside it. Verbatim quoted signal is exempt wherever it appears: it describes pain in the customer's words, and "slow" in a quote describes the pain rather than specifying the fix. Prose the author writes around a quote is theirs, not the customer's | S M L |
| 8 | Placeholders and open questions | No unresolved placeholder or template hint text, and three or fewer open questions. The cap is a handover gate: a fourth question means the ticket is still in discovery, not that the writing is wrong. An owner and a needed-by date per question were retired in v1.7 and are not required | M L |
| 9 | Experience reference | Any design reference states its precedence beside the link, illustrative or binding, and a `binding` one also names what was validated, with whom and when. Binding is earned: without that evidence the declaration fails, and the fix is to call it `illustrative`, which is the honest default and fails nothing. No reference at all is a valid state and passes: it says the solution space is still open. At L the Experience reference section exists and says which of the three it is. `experience_precedence` was retired as a frontmatter field in v1.7, so read the prose and never fail a ticket for the missing field. Where the retired field is still **present**, the prose is what is graded, and a field disagreeing with it is a stale-template finding in the shape check 10 uses, not a failure: a ticket whose prose declares a precedence while a leftover `experience_precedence: none` sits in the fields is carrying two answers, and the fields one is the dead one | M L |
| 10 | Tier | Content matches tier, per the v1.7 templates. S has Intent, Problem and evidence, and Behaviour with current and expected, Acceptance scenarios (happy path, error case, regression), an optional reference line, and nothing more. M has Intent, Problem and evidence, Outcome as prose, Scope, Decisions made as bullets, Acceptance scenarios, Open questions. L has Intent, Problem and evidence, Outcome and success criteria, Scope, the Product decisions table with rejected alternatives, Migration and rollback intent, Acceptance scenarios, Experience reference, Open questions, plus `decision_owner` and `outcome_metric` as fields. Any tier may end with Proposed terms (v1.14); it is never padding. Appetite, Ideas parked, a Pre-mortem and red-team section and a Decision owner section were retired in v1.7: their presence is a stale-template finding, not padding, and the fix is to fold the content into evidence and assumptions. `door` and `gate_decision` are gate fields at every tier since v1.9: absent on a pre-gate ticket is correct, absent on one that has passed the gate is a Note rather than a failure, since no check covers frontmatter completeness. A genuine mismatch is fixed by splitting or upgrading, never by padding. A decision written as `See PDR-nnnn` is complete when that file exists in the `decisions` folder; its rationale and rejected alternatives are read there, and a missing file fails | S M L |
| 11 | Consistency | No contradiction with CONTEXT.md capabilities, principles or non-goals, and no contradiction with an `accepted` PDR in the ticket's product area. A ticket that reverses an accepted PDR passes only when it says so and names the PDR it supersedes. A capability the ticket deliberately changes is not a contradiction: it passes when the ticket names the CONTEXT.md line it changes and says so in Assumptions and decided defaults, and it fails when the ticket has not noticed. Never demand the CONTEXT.md edit as a condition of handover: that file describes what we ship and is amended when the change ships. Silence does not fail; it is noted | S M L |
| 12 | Vocabulary | Every domain term appears in the engineering glossary with the meaning the ticket uses, or is defined under Proposed terms. A term the glossary lacks and the ticket does not define fails, and the smallest fix is its one-sentence definition there. A defined one passes and is noted as a glossary gap for engineering. A proposed term the glossary already has a word for fails as a synonym (fix: the glossary's word), and so does an existing term given a new meaning, which belongs in Open questions. Glossary unreachable: skipped, and skipped counts as failing | S M L |

Two notes on judgment:

- Check 5 is about binding language. "The Accounts table" is a product surface, allowed. "The accounts endpoint" is implementation, rewritten at the observable altitude or kept as a line marked non-binding.
- Check 11 reads CONTEXT.md's "Where this file is silent" section before noting a silence: one CONTEXT.md already admits is not news.
